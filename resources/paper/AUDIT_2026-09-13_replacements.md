## BLOCK A — §4.7–4.11 (lines 589–736)

### A1. [C1, medium] Ordering condition is misstated — line 599, §4.7
What is wrong: "The ordering matters physically whenever the slab Hamiltonians do not commute---an asymmetric profile together with a non-zero CP phase" gives, as the condition for non-commutation, something that is neither necessary nor sufficient. On any varying profile the slab Hamiltonians never commute (H_vac + V_j P and H_vac + V_k P commute only if V_j = V_k). What the CP phase controls is narrower: for a real Hamiltonian, reversing the profile transposes U, which leaves every survival probability unchanged and swaps P_{αβ} with P_{βα}; only with a complex H does reversal move the survival probabilities too.
Replacement (for the sentence beginning "The ordering matters physically"):
"The ordering matters whenever the slab Hamiltonians do not commute, which on a varying profile is always. What it changes depends on the Hamiltonian: for a real one, traversing the profile in reverse transposes the evolution operator, so every survival probability is unchanged and $P_{\nu_\alpha \to \nu_\beta}$ is swapped with $P_{\nu_\beta \to \nu_\alpha}$; with a non-zero CP phase the survival probabilities move as well. We check the order in the test suite against a two-slab case done in matrix arithmetic alone, with no quadrature involved."

### A8. [IMPORTANT, C1/C2] The 2π-per-slab seed is a phase criterion, and nothing reconciles it with "slab width follows the profile, not the phase" — line 620, §4.8 (with §4.5 and §4.2)
What is wrong: §4.5 tells the reader that the slab count answers to the profile and not to the phase. §4.2 states the convergence bound as a phase per slab (∫‖H‖ dl < π). §4.8 then seeds the ladder at "roughly 2π radians per slab", i.e. from the phase, and the ladder only refines upward from the seed. A fresh reader cannot reconcile these. The reconciliation is real and short: the phase enters once, as a floor on the count that the series' convergence requires; on the sweep of Fig. 2 the profile's floor (~150 slabs) lies above that seed until Φ approaches 10^3, which is why the count barely moves there; beyond it the seed grows in proportion to Φ, one slab per cycle against the ten or so steps per cycle a Runge–Kutta solver needs — and that is the regime §4.10 exists for. This also explains "the slab ladder can exhaust its cap" at line 662, which is otherwise unmotivated.
Replacement (for the "A physics-informed starting count" paragraph):
"\textit{A physics-informed starting count.}---Rather than start from one slab, we seed the ladder from an estimate of the accumulated traceless phase $\lVert \Omega_1 \rVert_2$ over the whole trajectory, aiming for roughly $2\pi$ radians per slab. This is the one place the phase enters the slab count, and it enters as a floor rather than as a cost: the series is guaranteed to converge only while the phase inside a slab is below $\pi$ (Section \ref{sec:method}), so the ladder starts from about one slab per oscillation and refines upward from there according to the profile. Across the sweep of \figu{phase_vs_profile} the profile's own floor of about $150$ slabs lies above that seed until $\Phi$ approaches $10^{3}$, which is why the count hardly moves there. Beyond it the seed grows in proportion to $\Phi$, and the advantage over a step solver reduces to one slab per cycle against the ten or so steps per cycle a Runge--Kutta method needs; Section \ref{sec:hybrid} is for that regime."
