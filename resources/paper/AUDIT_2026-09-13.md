- [ ] **74. ★★ [C1 — mechanism] The adiabatic-crossover explanation of the peak fits, but has not been separated from a purely numerical one that predicts the same peak** — lines 1433–1441.
As written the argument does not produce a peak: at a wide front the per-slab turn is small but there are proportionally more slabs, so "every slab is wrong by a little and the errors accumulate" gives a total independent of width. What makes the wide side cheap is that the per-slab errors carry the oscillation phase and cancel as a Riemann sum of an oscillating integrand; what makes the thin side cheap is that the phase barely advances, so a staircase reproduces the sudden projection at any step count. The peak sits where the phase advance across the front is of order one — which, for a front large enough to be a level crossing, *is* γ⁻¹ ~ 1. **So the physics reading is right, but the same data are equally consistent with a discretization reading** in which the peak marks where the caller's subdivision of the front hands over to the uniform grid (≈ 8L/N_bg ≈ 55 km for this dataset's background floor). One coincidence at one energy and one tolerance cannot tell them apart. See (b1).
